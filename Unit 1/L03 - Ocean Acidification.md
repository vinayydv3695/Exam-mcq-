# L03 — Ocean Acidification

![[assets/icons/03-ocean-acidification.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit1 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Definition
- **Ocean acidification** = ongoing decrease in ocean pH caused by uptake of anthropogenic atmospheric CO₂.
- The lecture states oceans absorb about **30%** of released CO₂ and about **22 million tons of human-created CO₂ per day**.

## Chemical mechanism
`CO₂ + H₂O → carbonic acid → H⁺ + bicarbonate`
- More **H⁺** lowers pH.
- H⁺ reacts with **carbonate ions**, reducing carbonate availability.
- Lower carbonate makes **CaCO₃ calcification** harder for shell- and skeleton-building organisms.

## Scale
- Historical ocean pH in the lecture: around **8.2**.
- Modern average surface pH: around **8.1**.
- Because pH is logarithmic, the lecture treats this as about a **25% increase in acidity**.
- Unmitigated emissions could lower ocean pH by another **0.5 units by 2100** in the lecture projection.

## Major causes
- Fossil-fuel combustion.
- Deforestation.
- Eutrophication and decomposition-driven local CO₂ increases.
- Land-use change.
- Natural inputs such as subsea volcanic activity.

## Impacts
- Thinner/brittle/dissolving shells and skeletons.
- Strong vulnerability of cold waters such as the Arctic.
- Physiological and reproductive stress.
- Coral reef and food-web disruption.
- Impacts on fisheries, shellfish and human coastal livelihoods.

## Case study: sea butterfly
- Tiny pteropod (**Thecosomata**).
- Important food source for fish, seabirds and baleen whales in the lecture.
- Reduced carbonate availability threatens shell formation.
- Supplied experiment: shells significantly dissolved in projected carbonate conditions within **48 hours**.

## Monitoring & solutions
- Multi-parameter observations.
- Global carbon measurements.
- Basin research cruises.
- Sensor moorings measuring seawater CO₂/pH and related variables.
- Lecture proposes renewable coastal energy, protective reef coatings, genetic research, underwater plant/carbon-sink strategies, and cheaper pH sensors.

## Exam memory
**CO₂ ↑ → carbonic acid ↑ → H⁺ ↑ → pH ↓ → carbonate ↓ → calcification ↓**
