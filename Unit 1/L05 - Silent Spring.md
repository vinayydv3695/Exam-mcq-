# L05 — Silent Spring

![[assets/icons/05-silent-spring.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit1 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Book & author
- **Silent Spring** — Rachel Carson, **1962**.
- Focus: harmful effects of indiscriminate chemical pesticide use, especially **DDT**.
- Title symbolizes a future without bird songs due to ecological degradation.

## Rachel Carson
- American marine biologist, conservationist and science writer.
- Used scientific evidence and accessible writing to show that pesticides can move through ecosystems and food chains.

## DDT
**Dichloro-Diphenyl-Trichloroethane** — synthetic organochlorine insecticide.
- Colourless crystalline solid; nearly odourless/tasteless.
- Highly effective insecticide.
- Chemically stable and persistent.
- Poorly soluble in water; highly soluble in fats.
- First synthesized **1874**.
- Insecticidal properties discovered by **Paul Hermann Müller in 1939**.
- Müller received the **1948 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine**.
- Used during WWII for malaria/typhus vector control; later widely used in agriculture and public health.

## Why DDT was initially valued
- Controlled disease vectors.
- Protected crops and increased productivity.
- Reduced economic losses.
- Relatively inexpensive.
- Long residual action reduced repeated applications.

## Main themes of Silent Spring
- Human activity can disrupt ecological balance.
- Pesticides can act as **biocides**, affecting non-target organisms.
- **Bioaccumulation:** toxin builds up within one organism over time.
- **Biomagnification:** toxin concentration increases at higher trophic levels.
- Environmental decisions should be based on science rather than commercial interests.
- Promote biological/sustainable pest control.

## Environmental effects
- Eggshell thinning in birds of prey.
- Declines in eagles, falcons, hawks and other predatory birds.
- Soil and aquatic contamination.
- Bioaccumulation and biomagnification.
- Reduced biodiversity.

## Human-health effects listed in the lecture
- Hypertension/high blood pressure.
- Liver damage.
- Possible carcinogenic effects.
- Hormonal disruption.
- Potential reproductive/developmental disorders.

## Criticism & legacy
Critics argued that reducing DDT could affect malaria control and agriculture, and accused Carson of overstating risks. The lecture states that later research supported many of her concerns.

Legacy: increased environmental awareness, tighter pesticide regulation, environmental legislation, influence on agencies such as the **U.S. EPA**, and multidisciplinary research.
- **Stockholm Convention on POPs:** adopted/signed 2001; entered into force 2004 (as stated in lecture).

## MCQ distinction
**Bioaccumulation = within one organism. Biomagnification = across trophic levels.**
