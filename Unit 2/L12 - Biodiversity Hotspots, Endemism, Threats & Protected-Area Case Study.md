# L12 — Biodiversity Hotspots, Endemism, Threats & Protected-Area Case Study

![[assets/icons/12-hotspots.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Hotspot criteria
- Official hotspot = region with **≥1,500 endemic vascular plant species** AND **≥70% loss of original primary habitat**.
- Lecture states 36 hotspots worldwide and 4 overlap India: **Himalayas, Western Ghats/Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Sundaland (Nicobar)**.

## Endemic species
- Restricted to one geographic location; found nowhere else.
- Lecture states roughly **60% of Indian endemic species** are concentrated in the Himalayas and Western Ghats.
- Risk increases with narrow range, isolation, habitat change, disease or invasive species.

## Important endemic examples
- Giant panda — China.
- Norway lemming — Norway.
- Scottish wildcat — Scotland.
- American alligator — Florida, USA.
- Poison dart frog — Costa Rica.
- Glacier bear — Alaska, USA.
- Sclater’s monkey — Nigeria.
- Kiwi — New Zealand.
- Indian examples: Asiatic lion, lion-tailed macaque, purple frog, Sangai deer, Hangul, Nilgiri Tahr, Malabar Civet, Indian rhinoceros.

## Main biodiversity threats
- Climate change.
- Deforestation and habitat fragmentation.
- Pollution.
- Overexploitation and poaching.
- Man–wildlife conflict.

## Threat status / documentation
- Lecture defines endangered species as those whose survival is seriously imperiled; it states population strength may have fallen from 50% to 5%.
- **IUCN Red List** catalogs threatened taxa.
- **Red Data Book** is described as historical documentation of rare/threatened species.

## Man–wildlife conflict
### Causes
- Shrinking forest boundaries due to agriculture/urban expansion.
- Human encroachment into wildlife territories.
- Injured/weakened wildlife moving outside natural ranges.
- Reduced natural prey base.

### Mitigation
- Habitat improvement: native plants + water sources inside reserves.
- Physical barriers: solar electric fencing and walls at high-risk edges.
- Technology: VHF collars, GPS and satellite tracking.
- Community training and dedicated response teams.

## Legal case study: 1998 Blackbuck poaching incident
- During filming of *Hum Saath Saath Hain*, Salman Khan was implicated in shooting two blackbucks at Kankani near Jodhpur.
- Lecture states charges included **Section 9/51 of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** for Khan; co-accused were also charged under Section 51 and IPC Section 149.
- Timeline in supplied material: 1998 report/custody; 2000 cognizance; 2006 conviction (5 years + ₹2,50,000); 2007 sentence suspension; 2012 fresh-trial charge stage; 2014 Supreme Court notice; 2016 Rajasthan High Court acquittal + state appeal; 2018 Jodhpur trial court guilty finding and prison sentence, with appeal filed.

## Exam traps
- Hotspot requires **both** plant endemism and habitat loss criteria.
- Endemic ≠ endangered: endemic describes geographic restriction; endangered describes conservation status/risk.
