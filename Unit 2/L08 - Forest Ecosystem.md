# L08 — Forest Ecosystem

![[assets/icons/09-forest.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Forest ecosystem
- Forest = natural, largely uncultivated terrestrial area outside human settlement; lecture gives Latin **foris = outside**.
- Forests are complex, layered and self-sustaining producer–consumer–decomposer systems.
- Multi-layered vegetation: **trees → shrubs → herbs → climbers**.
- Natural forest growth is irregular/random; plantations are generally more uniform.

## Types of Indian forests
### 1. Coniferous forests
- High-altitude Himalayan regions.
- Needle-like leaves with waxy cuticle.
- Conical form helps shed snow.
- Gymnosperms: **Pine, Deodar, Spruce, Fir**.
- Low plant diversity; thick-furred/migratory wildlife.

### 2. Broad-leaved forests
**Evergreen**
- High rainfall: Western Ghats, North-East India, Andaman & Nicobar.
- Dense canopy; gradual continuous leaf fall.

**Deciduous**
- Seasonal leaf fall in dry months.
- Rapid growth before monsoon.
- Dense herbaceous undergrowth.

**Thorn & shrub**
- Arid regions: Delhi, Punjab, Rajasthan, Gujarat.
- Examples: Bamboo, Babool, Ber, Neem, Keekar.
- Small/spiny leaves and deep roots.
- Fauna includes Chinkara, Blackbuck, Antelope.

**Mangrove delta forests**
- Intertidal/coastal deltas; example: Sundarbans / Ganga-Brahmaputra Delta.
- **Avicennia, Rhizophora, Sundari**.
- **Pneumatophores** and stilt roots; salt tolerance.
- Habitat of Royal Bengal Tiger.

## Threats
Urbanization, industrialization, mining, dams and population growth.

## Exam memory
- Conifer → needle + waxy + conical.
- Mangrove → intertidal + pneumatophores + salt tolerance.
