# L07P — Ecological Pyramids

![[assets/icons/08-ecological-pyramids.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Definition
- **Ecological pyramid** = graphical representation of trophic structure/function.
- Producers form the **base**; higher consumers are toward the apex.

## Trophic arrangement
1. Producers
2. Primary consumers (herbivores)
3. Secondary consumers (primary carnivores)
4. Tertiary consumers/secondary carnivores (apex)

## Shapes
- **Upright:** broad base → narrow apex.
- **Inverted:** narrow base → broader upper levels.
- **Spindle-shaped:** narrow base + apex, broad middle.

## Types
### Pyramid of Numbers
- Number of individuals at each trophic level.
- Can be inverted in a **parasitic food chain**; may be spindle-shaped in some terrestrial systems.

### Pyramid of Biomass
- Total mass/dry weight of living organisms at each trophic level.
- Usually upright in terrestrial ecosystems.
- Can be inverted in aquatic ecosystems.

### Pyramid of Energy
- Amount of energy at each trophic level + transfer losses.
- **Always upright** in the lecture framing.
- Linked to Lindeman’s 10% law.

## Ecosystem patterns from the lecture
| Ecosystem | Numbers | Biomass | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grassland | Upright | Upright | Upright |
| Forest | Spindle-shaped | Upright | Upright |
| Aquatic | Upright | Inverted | Upright |
| Desert | Upright | Upright | Upright |

## Exam traps
- **Energy pyramid = upright.**
- Aquatic **biomass** pyramid can be inverted.
- Forest **numbers** pyramid can be spindle-shaped.
