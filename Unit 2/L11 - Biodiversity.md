# L11 — Biodiversity

![[assets/icons/11-biodiversity.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Biodiversity
- Variation of life forms within an ecosystem, biome or the entire Earth.
- Important indicator of biological-system health.
- Lecture frames biodiversity as the product of ~4 billion years of evolution and essential for clean air, food and stable ecosystems.
- **May 22** = International Day for Biological Diversity.

## Three levels
1. **Genetic diversity:** variation in genes/genetic information within a species/population.
2. **Species diversity:** variety of species in a region.
3. **Ecological diversity:** variety of ecosystems/landscapes/biomes.

## India: biogeographic zones
The lecture states India has **10 biogeographic zones**. Key zones and examples:
- Trans-Himalaya — high-altitude cold desert; Chiru, black-necked crane.
- Himalayas — snow leopard, panda, wild sheep/goats.
- Indian Desert — wild ass, Great Indian Bustard, camel.
- Semi-Arid — Nilgai, Blackbuck, four-horned antelope, Asiatic lion, tiger, leopard.
- Western Ghats — high rainfall; Nilgiri Tahr, lion-tailed macaque, Grizzled Giant Squirrel.
- Deccan Peninsula — deciduous/thorn/semi-evergreen; source of major rivers.
- Gangetic Plains — fertile Ganges plains; rhino, elephant, wild buffalo.
- North-East India — transition zone; Eastern Himalaya hotspot.
- Coastal Region — mangroves, reefs, mudflats; crabs, turtles, tuna.
- Andaman & Nicobar — evergreen forests/coral reefs; high endemism.

## Biodiversity hotspots
A hotspot must satisfy BOTH:
- At least **1,500 endemic vascular plant species**.
- At least **70% loss of original primary habitat**.
- Lecture states **36** global hotspots and **4** involving India: Himalayas, Western Ghats + Sri Lanka, Indo-Burma, Sundaland (Nicobar).

## Endemism
- **Endemic species:** restricted to a particular geographical area and found nowhere else.
- Lecture: about **60% of India’s endemic species** are concentrated in the Himalayas and Western Ghats.
- Endemics are especially vulnerable because of narrow ranges/genetic isolation.

## Indian endemic fauna to memorize
- Asiatic Lion — Gir, Gujarat.
- Lion-tailed Macaque — Western Ghats.
- Purple Frog — Western Ghats.
- Sangai Deer — Keibul Lamjao National Park, Manipur.
- Kashmir Stag/Hangul — Dachigam & Chamba.
- Nilgiri Tahr — Nilgiri Hills/Western Ghats.
- Malabar Civet — Western Ghats.
- Indian Rhinoceros — Northern/North-Eastern India.

## Major threats
Climate change; deforestation/fragmentation; pollution; overexploitation/poaching; man–wildlife conflict.
