# L07 — Energy Flow in an Ecosystem

![[assets/icons/07-energy-flow.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Core idea
- **Energy flow** = transfer of energy through organisms via food chains and food webs.
- **Unidirectional:** energy moves one way through trophic levels.
- The **Sun** is the ultimate source for almost all ecosystems in the lecture.

## Trophic levels
1. **Producers:** plants, algae, phytoplankton.
2. **Primary consumers:** herbivores.
3. **Secondary consumers:** carnivores feeding on herbivores.
4. **Tertiary consumers:** higher/top carnivores.
5. **Decomposers:** break dead matter and recycle nutrients.

## Energy pyramid
- Shows energy available at successive trophic levels and transfer losses.
- Most accurate of the ecological pyramids because it directly represents energy transfer.
- Usually no more than **4–5 trophic steps** because usable energy declines rapidly.

## Lindeman’s 10% law
- Roughly **10%** of energy at one trophic level reaches the next.
- Remaining ~90% is used in respiration/metabolism/movement/hunting or lost as heat.

### Example
If producers have 80,000 kcal:
- Primary = 8,000 kcal
- Secondary = 800 kcal
- Tertiary = 80 kcal

## Bioaccumulation vs biomagnification
- **Bioaccumulation:** toxin builds up in one organism over time.
- **Biomagnification:** concentration rises at successive food-chain levels; top predators can have the highest concentration.
- Examples mentioned: DDT, mercury, PCBs.

## Fast recall
**Sun → Producers → Herbivores → Carnivores → Top carnivores**
**10% rule = move one trophic level = divide by 10**
