# L15 — GMOs and Bt Crops

![[assets/icons/15-gmo.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Core definitions
- **Biotechnology:** technological application using biological systems/organisms/derivatives to make or modify products/processes.
- **Genetic engineering:** direct manipulation of an organism’s genome, including insertion of donor DNA.
- **GMO:** organism/microorganism whose genetic material has been altered using genetic engineering.
- **GM food:** engineered crops for food/feed to express desired traits such as herbicide tolerance, insect resistance or improved nutrition.

## Traditional breeding vs biotechnology
- Traditional breeding can transfer **many unselected genes simultaneously**.
- Plant biotechnology is described as enabling a more targeted transfer of a desired gene.

## Bt crops
- Bt crops express insecticidal proteins derived from soil bacterium **Bacillus thuringiensis**.
- Lecture states Bacillus thuringiensis was discovered by **Ishiwatari in 1901**.
- These proteins are described as **Cry proteins / δ-endotoxins**.
- Target specific insect pests while the lecture states they are harmless to the human digestive system.

## Bt Cotton (India)
- Government approval for commercial use in India: **26 March 2002** for Mahyco in the lecture.
- India’s first commercial transgenic crop.
- Target: **cotton bollworms**.
- Benefit: reduced insecticide use and bollworm control.
- Key concern: resistance management.

## Bt Brinjal
- Gene used: transgenic **cry** gene insertion to target fruit and shoot borer in the lecture.
- Developer: Mahyco/Monsanto; licensed to public institutions including BARI in Bangladesh.
- USAID provided primary financial support for development in Bangladesh.
- First approved country in the lecture matrix: **Bangladesh (2013)**.
- India: moratorium/release denied.
- Main claimed benefit: reduced borer damage without sprays.
- Key concerns in matrix: toxicity concerns and premature plant mortality.
