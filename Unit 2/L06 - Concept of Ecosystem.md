# L06 — Concept of Ecosystem

![[assets/icons/06-ecosystem.svg|64]]

**Tags:** #unit2 #mcq #environmental-studies

## Ecosystem
- A **dynamic, self-regulating biological community interacting with its abiotic environment**.
- Can range from a small lawn to a biome.
- Ecosystems exchange energy and matter with surroundings; the lecture contrasts natural open systems with artificial closed systems such as **Biosphere 2**.

## Ecology
- From Greek **OIKOS** = house/place of living + **LOGOS** = study.
- Ecology studies patterns and interactions and uses ecosystems as units of study.

## Components
### Biotic
- **Producers/autotrophs:** manufacture organic food.
  - Photoautotrophs use chlorophyll and solar energy.
  - Chemotrophs obtain energy by chemical oxidation, including in dark environments.
- **Consumers/heterotrophs:**
  - Macroconsumers and microconsumers.
  - Herbivores = primary consumers.
  - Carnivores = secondary/tertiary consumers.
  - Omnivores include humans, foxes and birds.
- **Decomposers/saprotrophs:** fungi, bacteria, water molds and other decomposer organisms.
  - Use **absorptive nutrition** with extracellular enzymes.

### Abiotic
- Physical: solar flux, sunlight/shade, temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, water availability.
- Geographical: latitude, longitude, altitude.
- Chemical: organic and inorganic substances, salinity, toxicity.

## Thermodynamic laws
- **First law:** energy is conserved and transformed, e.g. solar → chemical → thermal energy.
- **Second law:** energy quality decreases and entropy increases; energy is lost at successive trophic transfers.

## Energy-flow models
### Single-channel
Linear flow involving light, heat, import/export and biomass storage.

### Y-shaped
Three linked pathways:
1. Photosynthesis pathway.
2. Grazing food-chain pathway.
3. Detritus food-chain pathway.

## Ecosystem classification
- **Natural:** aquatic (marine/freshwater) and terrestrial (forest/grassland/desert/tundra).
- **Artificial/anthropogenic:** crop fields, urban areas, industrial zones; may have high short-term output but depend on human management and can generate toxic by-products.

## Functional focus
- Structural complexity ↔ species richness ↔ energy throughput.
- Biogeochemical cycling.
- Food chains/webs, ecological pyramids, energy flow, nutrient cycles and ecological evolution.
